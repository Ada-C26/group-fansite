Badminton Fun Facts

Badminton is the fastest racket sport in the world, with professional smashes reaching speeds over 500 km/h (332 mph), easily beating the top speeds of tennis serves.

Fastest Smash: The recorded men's competition smash speed reached 565 km/h (and 438 km/h for women).

Second Most Popular Sport: It is ranked just behind football (soccer) as the second most popular participation sport globally, with hundreds of millions of regular players.

Extreme Match Lengths: Professional matches can be as short as 6 minutes or stretch out over 2 hours. The longest professional rally on record lasted for 211 shots.

High Physical Demand: Players sprint, lunge, and jump so much during a single match that they often run over 6 kilometers (nearly 4 miles) on a court smaller than a tennis court.

History

Ancient Roots: Simple versions of hitting a feathered projectile (known as battledore and shuttlecock) date back more than 2,000 years in ancient Greece, China, and India.

The Name: The modern game was shaped by British military officers in India who played a version called Poona. It got its current English name in 1873 when it was played at Badminton House in Gloucestershire, the estate of the Duke of Beaufort.

Olympic Debut: Badminton joined the Summer Olympic Games program in 1992 in Barcelona, where Asian nations have dominated the medal podium ever since.

Shuttlecocks and Equipment

Goose Feathers: Official tournament shuttlecocks are handmade using exactly 16 feathers—traditionally plucked from the left wing of a goose for consistent aerodynamic spin and flight stability.

Lightweight Projectile: A standard shuttlecock weighs between 4.74 and 5.5 grams—less than 5% of the weight of an iPhone.

Giant Shuttlecock: The Kansas City Museum houses the world's largest shuttlecock sculpture, built 48 times larger than normal.
